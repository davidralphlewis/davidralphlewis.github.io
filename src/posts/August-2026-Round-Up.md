The heat finally broke with rolling thunderstorms at the end of the month. August was a suspended month, a breath hold as wildfires raged nearby. 

I find it impossible to understand how anyone can deny climate change. This year has felt like the prophecy fulfilled, the results of our decisions coming home. 

I think a lot of the quote that went viral in 2022 (author unknown):

> Climate change will manifest as a series of disasters viewed through phones with footage that gets closer and closer to where you live until you’re the one filming it.

## Life

How cool was the eclipse? Even though it was only partial it still felt awesome in the old sense of the word - inspiring awe, something much larger than our small bodies. I went up a hill near me and chatted to a family who were very generous with their eclipse glasses. Thanks to them I saw when it hit 90%  which was magical.

[I've started jogging to my own great surprise](https://youtu.be/UGiQ_-Ktpvc?si=8IZDqWLHI_jmL7uC) I hate running, but need to do some cardio and often wake up too late to go to the gym. So I followed couch to 5k, somehow stumbling through to complete it. I still hate running, but at least I can go for half an hour now.

After living in our house for 9 months now, we are starting to make some changes. New doors and new table. It feels good to adapt our space to fit our needs.

## Books

Only one book completed this month because I'm reading four or five at once.

***Starve Acre*** by Andrew Michael Hurley is a folk horror novella about grief and how it can shatter people. What I enjoy about Hurley's writing is the taughtness, with not a moment wasted. This book is a clockwork tragedy, each element accelerating towards a conclusion that is both shocking and expected.

## Films

**Project Hail Mary** is a fun sci-fi film about a high school teacher encountering an alien in the depths of space. Alternating between preparation for the mission and the mission itself, its a fun film with some interesting science backing it. The visuals are often stunning, with practical effects for the alien giving it a weight that is rare. Maybe it owes a bit too much of a debt to *Contact* and *Interstellar*, but its a good film.

Speaking of Christopher Nolan, we went to see **The Odyssey** in the cinema. I thought it was well done and spectacular in a way many modern films aren't. Despite knowing the story, you really felt the peril and the tension of the journey. I'm glad I read Emily Wilson's translation earlier in the year. However, the overall message is a little muddled- in this version, Odysseus is a war criminal - but its OK because he feels bad about it - but then he does more murder? I wouldn't have minded if it made Odysseus an anti-hero, but the film tried to have it both ways. Still though, for a three hour film I found it zipped along.

## Music

Went to see **Jimmy Eat World** at Cardiff Castle. They're my wife's favourite band and usually tour smaller venues, so it was a delight to see them in a larger crowd. Having loads of people since along to the songs from *Bleed America* was a delight and felt euphoric.
