The slide into autumn was a welcome one. The heat of summer now forgotten, the darkness fell quickly. The trees have erupted in flame colours, a beautiful show. 

Maybe it's Halloween or maybe it's this slide into the dark nights, but I read and watched a lot of horror this October. More on that later.

## Life

I had ten days off work in the middle of the month, which was great.  We went up to the Peak District and walked along Bamford Edge and through Chee Dale. It was very needed to spend some time in nature and escape for a bit. The Peak District is beautiful and I feel like we barely scratched the surface.

After that, we came back home and I volunteered at Stroud's first poetry festival. There was a pretty good mix of different events and different voices. I even performed a poem in the open mic, for a first time in a long time. 

Then it was down to Hastings for a few days. My wife was doing a course and I had a few days mooching - reading books, writing, going to the sauna on the pier. Delightful. 

## Books

Lots of short books, lot of creepy reading for the season.

***The Lottery and Other Stories*** was a collection of stories by Shirley Jackson, including the infamous story *The Lottery*. The stories are reserved and strange, often being concerned with social anxiety instead of explicit creepiness. She's a keen observer of people and how little things can cause upset. There's no-one like her, I'd like to read more.

Also a collection of short stories, but that's about the only link, ***Exhalation*** by Ted Chiang is an incredible collection of stories that manage to be hard sci-fi whilst remaining human and beautiful. I loved the whole collection.

***A Guest in the House*** by E. M. Carroll was a graphic novel about a haunting that didn't quite work for me. The art was beautiful and parts of the story were creepy and compelling, but other parts remained slightly too ambiguous. This was especially apparent in the ending which left me confused more than scared.

***Piranesi*** by Susanna Clarke is a short novella and is absolutely incredible. I don't even really want to outline the premise, as part of the sublime joy of this book is finding it out as you go along. Just brilliant and I was thinking about it for a long time after. 

Back to the creepy, ***The Loney*** by Andrew Michael Hurley is his debut and still packs a punch. A church group goes on a trip to the country where they aren't wanted and some bad stuff ensues. Nominally folk horror, the terror also comes from the characters, from zealotry and a refusal to see the world as it is. One of his best. 

Similarly, ***Fever Dream*** by Samantha Schweblin (translated by Megan McDowell) got completely under my skin. A short novella narrated by a woman in a hospital bed, it feels like a dream you can't wake up from, a nightmare that never ends. Very different to Shirley Jackson or Andrew Michael Jackson, the horror here comes from the slow realisation of what is happening.

## Films

Sticking with Andrew Michael Hurley and because we were in the northern countryside for a bit, we watched the adaptation of *Starve Acre*. It deviates from the book a fair bit, which is good, but doesn't quite work as well in my opinion. Still some horrible images that stick with you and Matt Smith and Morfydd Clark are superb in the central roles.

We also saw *Wildwood* in cinemas, a fantastic stop motion fairy-tale love letter to Portland. Absolutely beautiful from start to finish, the animation in this is incredible, hand carved and intricate, a testament to what humans can make. The story itself is surprisingly dark in places, and it never pulls its punches. Really recommended, it might be one of my favourite things I've seen this year. 

## Theatre

More dark folk horror. We went to Bristol Old Vic to see *Lanny*, as we got cheap seats that turned out to be on the very front row. I enjoyed the book way back in 2019 and it was a good adaptation. Similar to *Starve Acre*, it concerns an elder god of nature taking a child. I thought the acting was excellent, especially the central couple, and the folk songs that were weaved throughout were suitably creepy and terrifying. The production also did a lot of interesting things with sound and shadow puppets, putting Dead Papa Toothwort of the edge of perception. 

## Music

I don't think I wrote about it when it came out, but I've been listening a lot to the new Phoebe Bridgers album *Lost Weekend*. It's really good, mixing her voice with electronics at the start, whilst being somehow sad and uplifting at the same time. 

My wife introduced me to Agnes Obel and her album *The Meaning of Flowers*
and I loved it immediately. Slow and quiet, with almost wordless songs, it's still emotional, intense and unlike anything else I've heard this year. 

Avalanches are back with *No Bad Memories*. I loved *We Will Always Love You* and this doesn't reach the same heights, but it does create a unique atmosphere of advert jingles and tv tunes that evokes a very specific time and place. A new Avalanches album is always welcome, especially after their long break.
