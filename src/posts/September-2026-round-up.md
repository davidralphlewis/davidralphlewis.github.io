Even now, seventeen years out from any educational setting, I think that September is the true start of the year. The sluggishness of summer is shrugged off and work can begin. It's a good time to take stock and figure out what I want to prioritise for the rest of the year. It also helped that the weather dropped from far too hot to  bearable but hot for this time of year.

## Life

I went to a small cabin the middle of nowhere by myself and wrote poems for a night. It was lush! I got quite a bit done, but it was more about having some space for myself to restart my practise. I've struggled to find time recently, but this showed me it was more about making time. I stopped on the way back in a beautiful sauna in the Welsh valleys, took time to stare out at nature and slow down. 

I spent some time ordering the weird little poetry chapbook / zine I've been working on. It's twelve poems about driving and death with a tentative title of *End of the Road*. It's too small to submit anywhere, and I've gone off submitting when I can do it myself, so I'll likely bring it together in a little zine shortly.

We also got new doors in the house and replaced the kitchen table. It's amazing what a difference these small changes make to the house and the living space. 

## Books

I read ***Luminous* by Silvia Park** for a sci-fi and fantasy book club I joined. Set in a future united Korea, it imagines a world where robots are a part of everyday life. It was OK, owing quite a lot to *Blade Runner*. The pacing is slightly off, with the first half being a bit of slog, but there's a lot in there about robots representing the other, about the subtleties of memory and forgetting to make it feel unique. The writing is also frequently gorgeous. 

I read Ursula K. LeGuin's translation of ***Tao Tse Ching* by Lao Tzu**. Not really knowing anything about Taoism, this was a delight. LeGuin's translations are clear and playful, and her commentary is sharp and full of wit. The poems themselves are full of contradictions, with a streak of distrust in rulers and reality throughout. It makes me want to go back and read *Earthsea* with this philosophy in mind. 

Speaking of seminal fantasy series, ***Assassin's Apprentice* by Robin Hobb** was a delight. Hobb uses all the tropes of fantasy - an imaginary kingdom threatened by outsiders, a mysterious power, court intrigue and rivalries- but it's all enlivened by a keen attention to detail and a focus on character. Hobb's characters feel emotionally real and complex, and she can write well to bring you into the story. Only another 15 books set in the same world.

In August I went to the book launch for ***The Revelation Tapes* by Stefan Mohammed** and finally got round to reading it. This is playful, exciting poetry set at some kind of apocalypse, using the language of the internet to craft a new poetics. There's a loose story following kids as the hope in the ruins and it never stops being inventive and interesting. Really great. 

Finally, I also enjoyed ***Helm* by Susan Hall**. A series of linked short stories about Britain's only named wind, this is fantastical nature writing that shows how humans deal with a phenomenon way more powerful than them. The wind is also given voice and is mischievous and curious, even when threatened by micro-plastics and climate change. A delight.

## Films

I only watched one film this month - **Iron Maiden: Burning Ambition**, a documentary looking back on the band's career. I didn't know a lot about Iron Maiden going in but really enjoyed this as an artefact of a long career and why fans are obsessed with them. The decision to only have the band in voice over was a slightly odd one, and the band did veer into *Spinal Tap* territory sometimes, but the quality of the music is undeniable.

## Music

A pub nearby put **Jeffrey Lewis** on for a gig. He was great, with songs about artistic failure and neuroses contrasted with comics on a projector. His DIY spirit is inspirational - it makes me want to get out there and make more things.
